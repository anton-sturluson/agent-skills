# Munger's Psychology of Human Misjudgment

Use this as a complete reference list, not a requirement to force every tendency into every report. In the bias audit, name only tendencies with a plausible causal role, explain the tempting conclusion, and state the countercheck. Reproduce only definitions actually used in the report appendix.

Munger described 24 causes in his 1995 talk and expanded the revised version published in *Poor Charlie's Almanack* to 25. The list and naming below follow the revised text published with permission at <https://fs.blog/great-talks/psychology-human-misjudgment/>.

1. **Reward and Punishment Superresponse Tendency:** Incentives strongly shape behavior and cognition, often causing people to rationalize whatever the reward system favors and overlook second-order damage.
2. **Liking/Loving Tendency:** Affection causes people to ignore faults, favor associated people or products, and distort facts to support what they like.
3. **Disliking/Hating Tendency:** Dislike causes people to ignore virtues, distort facts, and transfer hostility to associated people, arguments, or businesses.
4. **Doubt-Avoidance Tendency:** The mind rushes to remove uncertainty by reaching a decision, especially under pressure, confusion, or threat.
5. **Inconsistency-Avoidance Tendency:** People resist changing prior beliefs, habits, public commitments, and ownership decisions, even when new evidence warrants revision.
6. **Curiosity Tendency:** Humans naturally seek explanations and patterns; disciplined curiosity improves learning, while its absence leaves errors and incentives unexamined.
7. **Kantian Fairness Tendency:** People prefer conduct they would consider fair if everyone followed it and may react strongly when an arrangement violates that expectation.
8. **Envy/Jealousy Tendency:** Relative comparison can drive resentment, imitation, compensation demands, risk-taking, and capital-allocation decisions unrelated to intrinsic economics.
9. **Reciprocation Tendency:** People feel pressure to repay favors, concessions, gifts, and hostility, which can impair independent judgment and escalate conflict.
10. **Influence-from-Mere-Association Tendency:** Positive or negative feelings transfer from one person, outcome, price, brand, or event to something merely associated with it.
11. **Simple, Pain-Avoiding Psychological Denial:** When reality is emotionally painful, people may refuse to recognize or fully process it despite strong evidence.
12. **Excessive Self-Regard Tendency:** People overrate their own ability, decisions, possessions, organizations, and close associates while attributing success to skill and failure to circumstances.
13. **Overoptimism Tendency:** People systematically overestimate favorable outcomes and underweight delays, competition, execution risk, and failure rates.
14. **Deprival-Superreaction Tendency:** The prospect of losing something already possessed—or almost obtained—provokes a stronger reaction than an equivalent prospective gain.
15. **Social-Proof Tendency:** People imitate what others appear to believe or do, especially under uncertainty or stress, allowing consensus and crowd behavior to substitute for evidence.
16. **Contrast-Misreaction Tendency:** Judgment is distorted by comparison with an adjacent or recent reference point rather than by the thing's absolute economics.
17. **Stress-Influence Tendency:** Stress can narrow attention, intensify habit and social influence, and make ordinary judgment errors more severe.
18. **Availability-Misweighing Tendency:** Vivid, recent, repeated, or easily recalled evidence receives too much weight, while quiet or hard-to-imagine evidence receives too little.
19. **Use-It-or-Lose-It Tendency:** Skills, models, and judgment deteriorate without repeated use; fluency can also become outdated if practice is detached from feedback.
20. **Drug-Misinfluence Tendency:** Chemical dependence can overwhelm ordinary incentives and reasoning, causing denial, distorted judgment, and destructive behavior.
21. **Senescence-Misinfluence Tendency:** Aging can weaken memory, flexibility, and cognition unevenly; continuous learning and active practice may slow but not eliminate the effect.
22. **Authority-Misinfluence Tendency:** People defer to authority beyond the authority's competence and may continue following instructions despite contrary evidence or obvious error.
23. **Twaddle Tendency:** Humans generate and tolerate empty, distracting, or self-important talk that consumes attention without improving understanding.
24. **Reason-Respecting Tendency:** People comply more readily when given a reason—even a weak or irrelevant one—and may accept conclusions because an explanation sounds coherent.
25. **Lollapalooza Tendency:** Several tendencies acting in the same direction can reinforce one another and produce extreme outcomes that no single tendency would create.
